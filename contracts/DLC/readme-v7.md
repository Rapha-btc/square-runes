UASF = User Activated Soft Fork.

**What happened (2017):**

SegWit was ready but miners weren't signaling for it - some suspected they were blocking it to protect ASICBoost advantages. Core had the code, but activation was stalled.

A group of users said: we don't care what miners signal. On August 1st, our nodes will reject blocks that don't support SegWit. If you want to mine valid blocks for our chain, you comply.

They ran nodes with a flag called BIP148. The message was: we're the economic majority, we decide what Bitcoin is, not miners.

It worked. Miners capitulated and signaled for SegWit before the deadline rather than risk a chain split.

**The dynamic:**

Normally soft forks activate when miners signal readiness (miner activated soft fork / MASF). UASF flips this - users set a flag day, miners either comply or mine a minority chain.

**For OP_CAT:**

A UASF-style movement would mean: users run nodes that enforce OP_CAT starting block X, regardless of what Core merges or miners signal.

The risk: if not enough economic weight joins, you just fork yourself onto a minority chain. The power comes from credible commitment that enough exchanges, businesses, and users will follow.

It's a political weapon of last resort - "we are Bitcoin, come with us or get left behind."
