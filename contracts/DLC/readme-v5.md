OP_CAT would let Bitcoin verify ZK proofs natively - meaning trustless bridges to L2s without oracles or multisigs.

Today if you want to move BTC to another chain and back, someone has to sign. With OP_CAT, math signs instead.

Satoshi's original concern (memory exhaustion) is solved by the 520-byte stack limit added later.

"Bloat" concerns? Fees are low precisely because block space is underutilized. And users still choose whether to sign an OP_CAT tx or not - no one's forced into anything.

The real question isn't safety. It's whether Bitcoin should remain a static rock or become programmable sound money. I think the latter wins.
